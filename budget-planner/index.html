YNAB charges $14.99 a month for the privilege of telling your money where to go, and there is no free tier at all. The method it sells, zero-based budgeting, is not proprietary technology: you write down what you earn, you assign every dollar to a category before the month starts, and the difference between planned and actual tells you where the month went. It is a spreadsheet discipline, and charging rent for it every month is a choice, not a cost.

This planner implements the same discipline with no account and no subscription. Add your income, add your expense categories with a planned amount each, and log what you actually spend as the month goes on. The headline figure is the one that matters: remaining to assign, which is income minus everything you planned. When it reads $0.00, every dollar has a job. When it is positive, money is sitting unassigned; when it is negative, you planned more than you earn, and you know before the month starts rather than after.

Everything is saved in your browser’s local storage, and the tool says so plainly: saved in this browser only, no sync in v1. There is no server, no account, and nobody who can see your numbers but you. The tradeoff is honest too: clearing your browser data erases your budgets, and they do not follow you to another device. Every amount is stored and computed in integer cents, so the totals are exact, never $59.969999.

How to use it

  1. Use the month switcher above the planner to pick the month you want to budget. Each month keeps its own income and categories.
  2. Add your income entries: a label like "Paycheck" and the amount. Amounts accept $ signs and commas.
  3. Add expense categories with a planned amount for each: Rent, Groceries, Transport, whatever your month looks like.
  4. As you spend, type the actual amounts into the "Spent" box on each category. The planner shows what is left in each one and flags overspending.
  5. The charts update as you type: planned vs actual per category, and an income-vs-spending trend across your saved months once there are two.
  6. Watch the "Remaining to assign" figure. Move planned amounts between categories until it reads $0.00: every dollar assigned.
  7. Starting a new month? "Copy last month's plan" brings your categories and planned amounts forward with spending reset to zero. "Export CSV" downloads the month for your own records.
  8. Click "Load example" any time to see a filled-in month, or "Clear month" to start the current month over.

Common questions

Can I reuse last month's plan or export my budget?
Yes to both. "Copy last month's plan" copies the nearest earlier month's income and category plan into the current month with all spending reset to zero, so a new month starts planned but unspent. "Export CSV" downloads the current month as a spreadsheet-ready file, and "Copy summary" puts the headline figures on your clipboard. Nothing leaves your browser in any of these: the CSV downloads straight from this page.
What do the charts show?
Two things. "Planned vs actual" is one bar per category: the tick marks what you planned, the bar shows what you actually spent, and it turns red when you overspend. The trend chart plots income against spending across your saved months (up to 12) once you have two months of data, so you can see whether the gap is widening or closing.
Is this budget planner really free? What is the catch?
Yes, it is free, and the catch is that there is no sync between devices. The planner runs entirely in your browser and stores your budgets in local storage on your own device, so there is no server cost to recover and nothing to charge for. Paid budgeting apps charge a subscription because they sync your data and connect to your bank; this tool does neither, on purpose.
What does "remaining to assign" mean?
It is the core number in zero-based budgeting: your total income minus everything you planned to spend. If it is $0.00, every dollar has a job and your plan is complete. If it is positive, some income is still unassigned, so assign it to savings, debt, or a category. If it is negative, you planned to spend more than you earn, and you should trim categories before the month starts.
Where is my budget data stored? Is it private?
In this browser only, using local storage on your own device. Nothing is uploaded to any server, because there is no server involved, so nobody can see your numbers but you. The honest tradeoff: clearing your browser data erases your budgets, and they do not follow you to another computer or phone. There is no sync in v1, and the tool says so in the interface itself.
Can I budget for past or future months?
Yes. The month switcher moves one month at a time in either direction, and every month keeps its own income and categories. Past months stay as you left them, so you can look back at what you actually spent, and future months start blank until you plan them. There is no limit on how many months you keep.
How do I handle irregular income like freelance pay?
Add each payment as its own income entry when you know about it. Zero-based budgeting works fine with irregular income: assign the dollars you actually have, and when more arrives, add the entry and assign the new remainder. Do not budget money you have not received yet; plan with what is in hand and adjust when the next payment lands.
Are the totals exact?
Yes. Every amount is stored and computed in integer cents, so the classic floating-point problem where 0.1 + 0.2 = 0.30000000000000004 cannot happen. What you type is what gets added up, and the remaining-to-assign figure is exact to the cent. The display formats amounts with your browser’s standard currency formatting.
What happens if I overspend a category?
The category shows the overspend plainly, like "$24.50 over", and your "Left to spend" total drops accordingly. The planner does not move money on its own. The fix is the zero-based one: either reduce the planned amount in another category to cover it, or accept that the month costs more than planned and adjust next month’s plan.
Do I need an account to use this?
No, and there is no way to create one. Accounts exist so a service can store your data and charge you later; since your budget never leaves your browser, there is nothing an account would do. Open the page, plan the month, close the tab. Your budget is waiting when you come back on the same browser.
