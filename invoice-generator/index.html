FreshBooks charges $23 a month to let you invoice five clients. Invoice2go and its cousins run the same play: the editor is free, but exporting a clean PDF, removing their logo, or billing more than a handful of clients costs a subscription. For a freelancer billing three clients a month, that is a standing tax on getting paid. The pricing has nothing to do with the cost of generating an invoice, a document is a document, it is about knowing you will pay because your money is on the other side.

This generator removes the leverage. There is no account, no server, and no billing page, so there is nothing to meter and nothing to upsell. Your business details, client details, and past invoices live in your browser’s local storage, and the page says it plainly: saved in this browser only. The PDF is your browser’s print function: what you see in the live preview is exactly what prints, and no website can charge you for pressing Ctrl+P.

It does the unglamorous work correctly, which is the whole job for invoicing. Line items with quantity and rate, a percent or flat discount, tax applied after the discount, and every calculation done in integer cents so 3 × $19.99 comes out $59.97, never $59.969999. Invoice numbers auto-increment with your zero-padding preserved, 22 currencies are supported, you can drop in a logo, and the result looks like an invoice a client takes seriously rather than a spreadsheet printout.

How to use it

  1. Fill in your business details once: name, address, email, phone, and optionally a logo. They are saved in this browser and prefilled next time.
  2. Add the client, pick an invoice number (it suggests the next one automatically), and set the issue and due dates and currency.
  3. Add line items with the "Add line item" button: a description, quantity, and rate for each. Reorder with the arrows.
  4. Set your tax rate and any discount, either a percent or a flat amount. Add payment terms or bank details in the notes.
  5. Check the live preview beside the editor (or the Preview tab on a phone). It is styled like a finished invoice document.
  6. Click "Download PDF", choose "Save as PDF" in the print dialog, and send it. Click "Save to history" to keep a copy in this browser for your records.

Common questions

Is this invoice generator really free?
Yes. There is no free tier with a paid tier above it, no per-invoice fee, and no limit on how many invoices you make. The reason is structural: everything runs in your browser and the PDF comes from your browser’s own print function, so there is no server cost to recover. Invoicing tools that charge monthly are not charging for computation; they are charging because your revenue passes through them.
How does the free PDF download work?
Clicking "Download PDF" opens your browser’s print dialog with a stylesheet that prints only the invoice document, none of the page around it. Choose "Save as PDF" (or "Microsoft Print to PDF" on Windows) as the destination and save. It works in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari, and produces a clean PDF with selectable text rather than a blurry image.
Where are my invoices saved? Are they private?
In this browser only, using local storage on your own device, and it is stated in the tool itself. Nothing is uploaded to any server, because there is no server involved. The tradeoff: clearing your browser data erases your history, and invoices do not follow you to another device. Keep the downloaded PDFs as your permanent records.
How does the invoice number sequencing work?
When you save an invoice to history, its number becomes the last used, and the next invoice suggests the following number automatically. Prefixes and zero-padding are preserved: INV-0042 becomes INV-0043, and a plain 100 becomes 101. You can always override the suggestion and type any number you like.
Are the totals exact? I have seen rounding bugs in other tools.
Yes, by construction. Every amount is stored and computed in integer cents, so the classic floating-point problem (0.1 + 0.2 = 0.30000000000000004) cannot occur. A line of 3 × $19.99 is exactly $59.97, an 8.5% tax on $54.97 is rounded once to the cent, and the discount is clamped so it can never push a total below zero.
Can I use this for taxes in my country?
The tool gives you a tax percentage field and 22 currencies, which covers the mechanics of most invoices, but it does not know your local tax rules. Some regions require specific invoice fields, sequential numbering without gaps, or registered tax IDs. Treat the document as a starting point and check what your jurisdiction requires, or ask your accountant.
Can I add my logo to the invoice?
Yes. Upload a JPG or PNG and it appears at the top of the invoice next to your business name. The image is shrunk to a reasonable size before saving so it does not bloat your browser storage. It stays on your device with everything else and prints as part of the document.
Do I need an account to use this?
No, and there is no way to create one. Accounts exist so a service can store your data and charge you later; since your invoices never leave your browser, there is nothing an account would do. Open the page, fill in the invoice, download the PDF, close the tab.
