Looka will let you design a logo for free and then charge $20 or more to download it, which is a strange definition of "free logo maker". The logo is a few shapes and some text; there is no $20 worth of server computation in it. This tool stays on the free side of that line: you design, you download, the price is zero at every step.

Pick one of 18 simple stroke icons, a background shape (none, circle, rounded square, or badge), a font, and a color preset, and the logo renders as a real SVG, not a screenshot. Download it as a 512 or 1024 pixel PNG for social profiles and slides, or as the SVG itself for print and scaling. You can also save the finished logo straight to your business profile, so the invoice generator picks it up.

Honest scope: this is a wordmark maker, not a design agency. The icons are simple geometric strokes, the fonts are system fonts, and there are four background shapes. That is plenty for a side project, a market stall, a newsletter, or a prototype that needs to look like a real product. If your logo needs to be distinctive enough to trademark, hire a designer.

How to use it

  1. Type your business name and an optional tagline. They are the logo; everything else is framing.
  2. Pick an icon, or choose none for a pure wordmark. Then pick a background shape: none, circle, rounded square, or badge.
  3. Choose a font and a color preset. The preview updates as you click.
  4. Click "Save logo to business profile" if you want your other FreeKit business tools to use it automatically.
  5. Download the PNG at 512 or 1024 pixels, or grab the SVG for infinite scaling.
  6. If none of the icons fit your business, pick the None shape and the None icon for a pure wordmark with a strong color.

Common questions

Is the logo maker really free to download?
Yes, fully. Designing and downloading are the same step here: the PNG and SVG buttons save your logo with no paywall, no account, and no watermark. Looka charges $20 or more just to download what you already designed; that fee is the product being sold, not the design work. This tool skips the whole funnel and gives you the file.
What kind of logos can I make with it?
Simple wordmark-style logos: your business name in one of four fonts, optionally framed in a circle, rounded square, or badge background with one of 18 simple stroke icons (a star, a leaf, a compass, and friends). That is the whole scope, on purpose. It covers side projects, newsletters, stalls, and prototypes well. It does not do custom illustrations, gradients, or anything a trademark lawyer would call distinctive.
What do I get when I download? PNG or SVG?
Both are available. The PNG comes at 512 or 1024 pixels and is what you want for social profiles, slides, and documents. The SVG is the actual logo source, generated as clean vector markup, and it scales to any size without getting blurry, so it is the one to keep for print, signage, or handing to a designer later.
Who owns the logo I make?
You do. Everything is generated locally in your browser from templates, there is no account and no license agreement to click through. That said, simple geometric icons are not registrable as trademarks in most places, so if the logo will carry a real brand, treat this as a starting point and have a designer make it distinctive before you file anything.
What does "save to business profile" do?
It stores your finished logo, as a PNG image, in the shared business profile saved in this browser. Other FreeKit business tools, like the invoice generator, read that profile and pick up your logo and business name automatically, so you only set them up once. The profile lives in your browser only, like everything else here.
Can I use my own fonts or upload an image?
Not in v1. The four fonts are system fonts that render identically everywhere, which is why the logo looks the same on your machine and the download. Custom font uploads would need the file, and image uploads would make it an image editor rather than a logo maker. The tight scope is deliberate: simple wordmarks, done well.
Why only 18 icons? Can I request more?
The icons are hand-drawn SVG strokes designed to stay legible at small sizes, and a small set keeps the tool fast and the choices manageable. Eighteen covers the common cases: food, nature, tech, travel, pets, music, and a few abstracts. If nothing fits, pick None for both the shape and the icon: a pure wordmark is often the strongest choice anyway.
Do I need an account?
No. There is nothing to sign up for and no way to sign up. Your logo settings are saved in this browser automatically, and the downloads are files on your device. The whole point of the tool is that a $20 download fee for a text logo is silly, and an account would be the first step toward it.
