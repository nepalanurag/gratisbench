What these tools do

YNAB charges $14.99 a month for zero-based budgeting, and Rocket Money charges for showing you your own subscriptions. Both are arithmetic a browser does for free. The budget planner covers the first job: give every dollar a month a job, track planned spending against actual spending per category, and see what is left to assign. The subscription tracker covers the second: list every subscription with its billing cycle and date, see the true monthly and yearly totals, and get renewal reminders computed on the page.

Both tools are honest about what they cannot do. There are no bank connections, so you type your numbers in by hand, and there are no push notifications, so renewal reminders live on the page where you can see them. In exchange there is no account, no fee, and no server holding your financial life: everything is saved in this browser only.

Common questions

Are these money tools free?
Yes. Both are free with no limits and no account. There is no premium tier, no sync service, and no per-budget fee. The math, adding cents and counting days to a date, runs entirely in your browser.
Where is my financial data stored?
In your browser only, using local storage on your own device. Nothing is uploaded to a server, because there is no server. Clearing your browser data erases it, and it does not sync between devices. For money details, local-only is a privacy feature.
Do the tools connect to my bank?
No. Bank connections are what the paid apps charge for, and they are also the thing that makes your financial data live on someone else’s server. You type your income and spending in by hand, which takes a few minutes a month and keeps everything local.
Will I get reminders before subscriptions renew?
On the page, yes: the subscription tracker shows an “Upcoming renewals” section computed from your saved dates every time you open it. It cannot send push notifications or emails in v1. For a renewal that would cost you real money to miss, put it in your calendar too.
