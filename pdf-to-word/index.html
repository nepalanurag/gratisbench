Adobe puts PDF-to-Word export behind Acrobat Pro, a paid subscription, and even the free web version asks you to sign in to an Adobe account before it hands you the file. That is not because the conversion is expensive to run; it is because a useful feature makes a good reason to collect your email address. Reading the text out of a PDF and writing it into a Word document is a modest local computation, and it does not need your identity attached.

This converter does the whole job inside your browser. It reads your PDF locally, groups the extracted text into paragraphs, detects headings by font size and gives them real Word heading styles, and embeds the document’s images inline in reading order. The result is a genuine .docx file that opens in Word, Google Docs, or LibreOffice, ready to edit. Nothing is uploaded anywhere, which also makes it safe for contracts and other documents you would never send to a stranger’s server.

Now the honest part, stated plainly: this tool extracts text and images into an editable Word document. Complex layouts, tables, and multi-column designs will not be preserved. What you get is a clean single-column document with your words, your headings, and your images, not a pixel-perfect replica of the original page. If you need every table cell and text box exactly where it was, use desktop software. If you need the words editable, this is the fast free route.

How to use it

  1. Drop your PDF onto the box above, or click it to browse. The file never leaves your device.
  2. Click "Convert to Word". The PDF is read page by page in your browser; headings and images are picked up as it goes.
  3. When the conversion finishes, the results list how many paragraphs, headings, and images were extracted.
  4. Download the .docx file. It opens in Microsoft Word, Google Docs, or LibreOffice.
  5. Give the document a quick read. Headings use real Word styles, so the navigation pane and table of contents work; fix any heading the size heuristic missed.

Common questions

Is this PDF to Word converter really free?
Yes. The conversion runs entirely in your browser using code that ships with this page, so there is no server cost to recover and nothing to charge for. There is no account, no daily quota, and no watermark in the Word document.
What exactly do I get in the Word document?
An editable .docx with your PDF’s text grouped into paragraphs, headings detected by font size and saved as real Word heading styles, and the document’s embedded images placed inline in reading order. What you do not get: the original layout. Tables, multi-column designs, and precise positioning are not preserved; the output is a clean single-column document.
Why are tables and complex layouts not preserved?
A PDF does not store paragraphs or tables; it stores individual characters placed at exact positions on the page. Rebuilding tables and multi-column flow from those positions reliably is the hard part of conversion, and it is where desktop software earns its price. This tool is honest about the boundary: it extracts the content faithfully and does not pretend to rebuild the design.
Is my PDF uploaded to a server?
No. The PDF is read locally, the text and images are extracted locally, and the Word document is assembled locally. Your file never leaves your device, which makes this suitable for contracts, financial documents, and anything else you would not upload to a free converter site.
What happens with a scanned PDF?
A scanned PDF is pictures of pages, not text, so there is no text layer to extract. The converter still runs and embeds any images it finds, but the results will tell you plainly how many pages had no extractable text. For scans, run the pages through an OCR tool first and convert the recognized text instead.
How are headings detected?
By comparing font sizes across the whole document. Text set clearly larger than the body size becomes a heading, with three levels for large, medium, and small headings, plus short bold lines. Because it is a heuristic, always skim the result: a missed heading takes two seconds to fix with Word’s styles, and the real styles make the navigation pane work.
Is there a page limit?
The first 200 pages of a PDF are converted, which covers reports, contracts, theses, and books in the common case. Very large documents are cut off there to keep the conversion fast and memory-safe in a browser tab. If your document is longer, split it first with the split tool and convert the parts.
Can I convert the Word document back to PDF?
Yes, and you do not need a tool for it. Word, Google Docs, and LibreOffice all have a built-in Save as PDF or Print to PDF option. Edit the .docx this converter gives you, then export to PDF from the editor when the text is final.
